# Ω-11 — Potential ablation: does structure emerge without a built-in double well?

**Date:** 2026-10-09  
**Status:** Nine numerical runs executed; results limited to a dimensionless toy model.

## Question

Does a small random field grow into large-amplitude domains under several potential choices, or does that behavior depend on the potential?

## Models

All runs use the 1D periodic equation \(u_{tt}=u_{xx}-dV/du\), same band-limited random initial field (RMS 0.03), seed 20261013, L=80, T=20, 40 Fourier modes, and three grid sizes.

- **Double well:** \(V=(u^2-1)^2/4\). Its \(u=0\) state is linearly unstable; preferred states are ±1.
- **Single well:** \(V=u^2/2\). The origin is stable.
- **Soft quartic:** \(V=0.025u^4\). The origin is also stable, with no quadratic mass term.

## Main results

| Potential | N | RMS at t=0 | RMS at t=20 | Fraction near ±1 at t=20 | Relative energy drift |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Double well | 256 | 0.030 | 0.893 | 39.5% | -0.505% |
| Double well | 512 | 0.030 | 0.903 | 40.6% | +0.0140% |
| Double well | 1024 | 0.030 | 0.903 | 41.5% | +0.0592% |
| Single well | 256 | 0.030 | 0.0195 | — | +7.61% |
| Single well | 512 | 0.030 | 0.0192 | — | +1.05% |
| Single well | 1024 | 0.030 | 0.0195 | — | +0.112% |
| Soft quartic | 256 | 0.030 | 0.0201 | — | +7.89% |
| Soft quartic | 512 | 0.030 | 0.0201 | — | +1.56% |
| Soft quartic | 1024 | 0.030 | 0.0202 | — | +0.377% |

Energy drift is resolution-sensitive for the control potentials, especially at N=256. Interpret coarse-grid runs cautiously. The double-well results are qualitatively consistent across all three resolutions; RMS rises to about 0.9 and around 40% of grid points lie near ±1.

## Interpretation

1. **The large-amplitude domain pattern is specific to the double-well model in this test.** The single-well and soft-quartic controls remain small.
2. This is expected from the equations: around \(u=0\), the double-well force contains a linear growth term because \(-u(u^2-1)=u-u^3\). The origin is unstable, so fluctuations grow. In the single-well model the force is \(-u\), which restores small fluctuations.
3. Therefore the experiment does **not** demonstrate spontaneous structure from relation alone. The decisive behavior is already encoded in the double-well potential.
4. The number of sign crossings is not by itself a good measure of stable structures: the controls have many sign crossings despite small amplitudes.
