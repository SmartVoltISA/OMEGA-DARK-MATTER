# Ω-14 — Multi-domain persistence and network controls

**Date:** 2026-10-09  
**Status:** 48 numerical runs executed; 12 per condition.

## Question
Do adaptive couplings create persistent multi-part organization beyond fixed couplings, or does ordinary coupling alone explain the observed order?

## Protocol
- 120 binary-state nodes on a ring-lattice with random rewiring (probability 0.15; nominal k=8).
- 800 synchronous stochastic updates; first 400 steps discarded; metrics sampled every 20 steps.
- Temperature 0.45; initial edge weights uniform on [0.7, 1.3].
- Adaptive update: (w_{ij}\leftarrow\mathrm{clip}(w_{ij}+0.008(s_i s_j-0.1w_{ij})-0.003(w_{ij}-1),0.05,2)).
- Four conditions: adaptive weights, fixed weights, weights randomly reassigned to edges each step, and no coupling.
- 12 seeds per mode (20261020–20261031).

## Results (means over runs)

| Mode | Edge agreement | Polarization | Largest same-sign component | Lag-100 persistence |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Adaptive | 0.9700 | 0.8245 | 0.9123 | 0.9990 |
| Fixed | 0.9715 | 0.9710 | 0.9855 | 0.9713 |
| Shuffled weights | 0.9705 | 0.9699 | 0.9850 | 0.9704 |
| No coupling | 0.4994 | 0.0726 | 0.5332 | 0.5049 |

Adaptive weights became more heterogeneous (mean final standard deviation 0.1831 vs 0.1725 for fixed initial weights), but did not improve edge agreement or largest-component size relative to fixed coupling. Adaptive mode had lower global polarization and high lag-100 persistence; high persistence may indicate freezing into a metastable state, not richer organization.

Fixed and shuffled-weight controls were nearly indistinguishable on major order metrics. Generic coupling appears sufficient to create high agreement in this model.

## Interpretation and limitations

**Ω-14 does not support the claim that adaptive relational changes outperform fixed couplings at generating persistent multi-part structure.** It supports the narrower claim that coupling drives organization relative to no coupling.

- Toy binary-spin model, not a physical theory of matter.
- Largest component depends on the chosen graph.
- Ring-domain count follows node index order and is secondary for a rewired graph.
- High persistence may mean freezing rather than robust self-maintaining organization.
- This is not a test of superluminal information propagation.
