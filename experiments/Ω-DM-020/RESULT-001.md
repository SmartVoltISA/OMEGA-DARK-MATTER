# Ω-DM-020 — RESULT-001

Status: INCONCLUSIVE / PARTIAL SUPPORT

## Local execution

Random symmetric positive nearest-neighbour relational operators were tested in 1D, 2D and 3D. The measured observable was the radial gradient of the shell-averaged environmental response after a localized intervention.

Three independent realizations were used for each dimension in this exploratory run.

### Fitted exponents

1D:

`p = 0.113, 0.019, 0.309`

The 1D response is not well described by a radial power law in this finite setup; fit R² values were low/moderate. This is consistent with the expected marginal behavior but is not a quantitative confirmation.

2D:

`p = 0.934, 0.838, 0.927`

Mean `p ≈ 0.900`.

3D:

`p = 1.762, 1.756, 1.767`

Mean `p ≈ 1.762`.

The 2D and 3D results move in the expected dimensional direction, but the 3D exponent is below 2 and the 1D measurement is poorly conditioned.

## Interpretation

The experiment provides partial evidence that dimensionality controls the radial form of the environmental relational response, but it does not yet establish the exact dimensional law `p=d-1`.

The result must NOT be used as evidence for physical gravity. The current operator is still a deliberately chosen local symmetric network, and finite boundaries/random weights affect the estimate.

## Next correction

Repeat with substantially larger domains, matched physical radius in lattice units, controlled anisotropy statistics, explicit confidence intervals, and both uniform-weight and random-weight ensembles. Add a flux-conservation diagnostic through shells. The key question is whether constant outward relational flux is sufficient to force the dimensional law without prescribing the exponent.
