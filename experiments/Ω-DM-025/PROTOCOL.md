# Ω-DM-025 — Conservation from Elementary Local Exchange

## Question
Can conservation arise from a primitive relation rule itself, rather than being imposed as a Laplacian/balance axiom?

## Primitive rule
The environment is a lattice of relational states u_i. An elementary interaction event on a neighboring pair (i,j) transfers an amount proportional to their relational difference:

Δu_i = -J_ij
Δu_j = +J_ij

The pair update is exactly antisymmetric, so total interior quantity is conserved event-by-event before any continuum or operator representation is introduced.

For the static response, repeated relaxation of these elementary exchanges is equivalent to reaching a zero-net-exchange state. Edge strengths are heterogeneous and generated from local random traits. No radial kernel or inverse-square law is used.

## Tests
A. 3D conserved elementary exchange, six seeds, N=21.
B. Source amplitudes 0.5, 1, 2.
C. Leakage control: add independent local loss after exchange.
D. Measure shell flux directly from elementary pair exchanges and radial gradient exponent p.

## Hypothesis
H1: If conservation is generated at the elementary relation event level, the macroscopic response approaches the geometric law p=d-1 without explicitly imposing a Laplacian conservation operator.

H0: Elementary local exchange does not robustly produce the geometric scaling.

## Decision
For conserved runs require near-constant shell flux and mean |p-(d-1)| <= 0.15. Leakage must measurably destroy constant flux and alter the response.

## Boundary
A PASS demonstrates emergence of conservation from an antisymmetric local exchange rule and its geometric consequence. It does not establish physical gravity/dark matter.
