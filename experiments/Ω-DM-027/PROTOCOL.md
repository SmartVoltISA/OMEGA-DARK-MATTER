# Ω-DM-027 — Minimality of the inverse-square mechanism

## Question
After Ω-DM-026 showed that locality + antisymmetric exchange + conservation do not generically give p≈2 on irregular nonlinear media, what additional condition is actually responsible for the earlier p≈2 result?

## Minimality ladder
We compare three model classes without inserting a 1/r² law:

A. local antisymmetric exchange only;
B. A + linear constitutive response;
C. B + isotropic 3D shell measure / radial coarse-graining.

The target is not to assume gravity. The target is to identify the smallest structural condition that makes constant conserved flux imply a radial gradient proportional to 1/r².

## Analytic criterion
For a conserved radial flux Q(r)=const through a family of isotropic spherical shells, if shell measure scales as S(r) proportional to r², then the radial flux density satisfies G(r)=Q/S(r) proportional to r^-2.

This is a geometric consequence of conservation plus 3D isotropic shell measure. It is not a derivation of physical gravity.

## Empirical component
Use Ω-DM-021/022 as the controlled conserved local-exchange reference and Ω-DM-026 as the irregular nonlinear counterexample. Recheck that the p≈2 result depends on the shell geometry/coarse-graining assumption rather than merely on antisymmetric exchange.

## Acceptance
H1: the minimal sufficient structural statement is conservation + 3D isotropic shell measure; constitutive linearity is not itself sufficient, and antisymmetry alone is not sufficient.
H0: another untested structural ingredient is required.

## Scope
Model-level mathematical mechanism only. No claim about physical gravity, dark matter, or a measured gravitational law.
